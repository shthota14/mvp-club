Top 10 Practical Guides
Every Tech Founder Needs

Original, founder-tested guides covering the full journey — from choosing a co-founder to scaling without chaos. No theory. No fluff.

01
Before you code
How to Validate an Idea
Focus on evidence that people care before investing time and money.
→
Identify a specific customer problem
→
Interview 20–50 potential users
→
Understand current workarounds
→
Test demand with prototypes or landing pages
🎯
Customers describe the problem in their own words.
02
The holy grail
How to Find Product-Market Fit
PMF happens when customers consistently find value and keep returning.
→
Are users actively and repeatedly using it?
→
Would they be disappointed if it disappeared?
→
Are referrals happening naturally?
→
Is retention improving over time?
🎯
Solve one painful problem extremely well before expanding features.
03
Ship faster
How to Build an MVP That Matters
An MVP is not a smaller product. It's the simplest way to test your core assumption.
✓
Build only essential functionality
✓
Ignore edge cases initially
✓
Prioritise learning over perfection
✓
Launch sooner than feels comfortable
🎯
The MVP's job is to generate insights, not impress investors.
04
Choose wisely
How to Choose a Co-Founder
A co-founder relationship often lasts longer than many marriages. Evaluate carefully.
→
Complementary skills, not identical ones
→
Shared values on risk, money, and pace
→
Work ethic compatibility
→
Ability to handle disagreement productively
🎯
Work together on a meaningful project for months before formalising anything.
05
Do things that don't scale
How to Get Your First 100 Customers
Early growth is manual. Persistence beats scalable marketing channels every time.
→
Direct outreach to your network first
→
Community engagement in your niche
→
Personalised demos and onboarding
→
Ask every user for one referral
🎯
Customers arrive through recommendations you didn't engineer.
06
Don't guess
How to Price Your Product
Pricing affects growth, positioning, and profitability. Don't base it on development costs.
→
Value delivered to the customer
→
What alternatives exist and what they cost
→
Willingness to pay (ask directly)
→
Cost structure and target margin
🎯
Customers pay without negotiating the price down significantly.
07
Only if you need it
How to Raise Funding
Fundraising should support a strategy, not replace one. Most founders bootstrap longer than expected.
→
Clear vision that investors can believe in
→
Evidence of traction — real numbers
→
Market understanding and sizing
→
Strong narrative, not just a pitch deck
🎯
Investors say yes to the team as much as the idea.
08
Hire slow
How to Build an Effective Team
Early hires shape company culture and execution quality for years.
✓
Adaptability over experience
✓
Ownership mindset — no hand-holding
✓
Strong communication skills
✓
Mission alignment — they care about the problem
🎯
A small team of exceptional people outperforms a large average one.
09
Cut the noise
How to Measure What Matters
Track a small set of metrics aligned with your business model — nothing else.
→
Customer acquisition cost (CAC)
→
Monthly recurring revenue (MRR)
→
Retention and churn rate
→
Customer lifetime value (LTV)
🎯
Avoid vanity metrics that look impressive but don't change decisions.
10
Amplify what works
How to Scale Without Losing Focus
Growth creates complexity. Scaling should amplify what already works — not fix what doesn't.
✓
Clear priorities — say no to most things
✓
Document processes before expanding the team
✓
Disciplined hiring — roles with clear ownership
✓
Keep customer feedback loops short
🎯
Growth increases without operational chaos.

The Founder Operating Principles

Consistent habits across every stage that successful founders share

01
Talk to customers constantly — not just at the start.
02
Solve real problems, not just interesting ones.
03
Launch earlier than feels comfortable. Done beats perfect.
04
Learn faster than competitors. Speed of learning is your moat.
05
Prioritise retention before chasing growth.
06
Hire slowly and intentionally. Every early hire shapes culture.
07
Protect cash. It is your strategic oxygen.
08
Measure outcomes, not activity. Busy ≠ moving forward.
09
Stay adaptable. The plan will change — your judgment shouldn't.
10
Keep building value for customers. Revenue follows value.
