How to Validate
an Idea

Stop guessing. Start learning. Here's the exact process to find out if people will actually pay for what you're building — before you build it.

The 5-step validation flowchart
🎯
Step 1
Get crystal clear on the problem
Before talking to anyone, you must be able to describe the problem in exact, specific terms. Vague problems get vague validation.
Write this down first:
"The problem is [X]. It happens when [Y]. Right now people deal with it by [Z]."
Warning sign:
If your problem statement is more than 2 sentences, it's not specific enough yet.
🗣
Step 2
Find 5–10 people who have the problem
Not your mum. Not your friends. Find strangers who match your ideal customer profile — they'll give you honest, unbiased feedback.
Where to find them:
Reddit communities · LinkedIn DMs · Facebook groups · Industry forums · Slack communities · Meetups
Your message:
"Hi, I'm researching [problem]. Would you spare 15 mins for a chat? No pitch — I just want to learn."
📋
Step 3
Run the interview — ask these 5 questions
Keep it conversational. Record it (with permission). Never mention your solution until the end.

🎙 The 5 core interview questions

1
"Tell me about the last time you experienced [problem]. What happened?"
2
"How do you deal with it today? What tools or workarounds do you use?"
3
"What's the most frustrating part of your current solution?"
4
"If you could wave a magic wand and fix this, what would the perfect solution look like?"
5
"How much does this problem cost you — in time, money, or stress — per month?"
🔍
Step 4
Spot the signal — not the noise
Not everything people say is validation. Here's how to tell the difference between real signal and polite noise.

✅ Real signal — green flags

🟢 "I've already tried to solve this myself"
🟢 "I'd switch from [current tool] for this"
🟢 "I'd pay £X/month for this without hesitation"
🟢 "Can I be a beta user? Here's my email."
🟢 "This happens to me at least once a week"

🚩 Noise — red flags

🔴 "Yeah that sounds like a good idea"
🔴 "I might use something like that someday"
🔴 "My friend would definitely want this"
🔴 "Sure, I'd probably sign up if it was free"
🔴 Any answer that avoids committing to anything
🚦
Step 5
Make the go / pivot / stop decision
After your 5–10 interviews, you should have enough data to make a clear call.
🟢 Go — build it
You found the signal
✓ 3+ people confirmed the pain
✓ At least 2 said they'd pay
✓ You understand the job-to-be-done
🟡 Pivot — adjust
Mixed signals
↺ Reframe the problem statement
↺ Target a different customer segment
↺ Test a different solution approach
🔴 Stop — move on
No real signal
✗ Nobody actually has the problem
✗ Existing solutions work fine
✗ No one would pay even a small amount
⚡

The validation mantra: "Fall in love with the problem, not your solution"

Most founders do validation wrong — they pitch their idea and ask "do you like it?" That's not validation. Real validation is understanding the problem so deeply that the solution becomes obvious. If you talk to 10 people and you still love your original idea, you probably weren't listening hard enough.
