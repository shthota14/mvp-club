Money & Markets

Understanding the numbers behind your business

01
Market sizing
TAM, SAM & SOM
Investors want to know the size of the prize. Wrong sizing kills deals before they start.
T
TAM (Total Addressable Market): everyone who could ever buy this
S
SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market): who you can realistically reach
S
SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market): who you'll actually win in 3–5 years
🎯
Investors fund SOM, not TAM. Know all three and be able to defend your SOM number.
02
Monetisation
Revenue Models
How you charge determines your growth curve, CAC, and LTV. It's a strategic choice, not a default.
→
SaaS subscription: predictable MRR, high retention value
→
Usage-based: scales with customer success, hard to forecast
→
Marketplace/transaction: takes a % of GMV, needs volume
🎯
Match your revenue model to your customer's buying behaviour, not what sounds good in a pitch.
03
Core metrics
Unit Economics
CAC, LTV, and payback period: if these don't work, no amount of growth will save you.
$
CAC: all acquisition spend ÷ new customers acquired
$
LTV: avg revenue per user × gross margin × avg lifespan
$
LTV:CAC should be 3:1 minimum. Below 1 = burning money
🎯
Know your payback period: how many months until one customer pays back their acquisition cost?
04
Fundraising
Startup Valuation
Valuation is a negotiation, not a calculation. It's based on traction, team, market, and narrative.
→
Pre-revenue: story, team, and market size drive valuation
→
Post-revenue: multiples of ARR (typically 5–15× at Series A)
→
Pre-money vs post-money: know which you're discussing
🎯
The best way to raise at a higher valuation is to not need the money. Traction is your leverage.
05
Funding
Bootstrapping
Growing with your own revenue keeps you in control and forces the discipline investors can't teach you.
✓
No dilution — you keep 100% equity until you choose otherwise
✓
Customer revenue as validation: if they pay, you're solving something real
✓
Slow but sustainable — you control pace, direction, and exit
🎯
Bootstrapping to PMF, then raising, maximises your valuation and negotiating power.
06
Funding
Angel Investing
Angels are high-net-worth individuals who invest personal capital in early startups, often for equity.
→
Typical cheque: £10k–£150k. Less due diligence than VCs
→
Best angels bring domain expertise and warm introductions
→
Find via AngelList, Sifted, Founder network, YC alumni
🎯
The best angels have operated before. Their introductions and advice are worth more than their cheque.

Deals & Legal

What you sign when money changes hands

07
Legal
Term Sheets
A term sheet sets out the key economics and governance of an investment. Read every word.
!
Valuation cap: maximum company value used to convert notes
!
Liquidation preferences: who gets paid first in a sale
!
Pro-rata rights: investor's right to join future rounds
🎯
Valuation and board control matter most. Don't accept 1× non-participating preference without understanding it.
08
Equity
Stock Options & ESOPs
Equity is how you compete with big companies for talent. Structure it right or lose your best people.
→
ESOP pool: typically 10–20% reserved for employee equity
→
Vesting: 4 years with 1-year cliff is the standard schedule
→
Options vs shares: options give the right to buy at a strike price
🎯
Create the ESOP pool at incorporation. Doing it at Series A dilutes founders, not investors.
09
Equity
Equity Splits & Cap Tables
How equity is divided between founders shapes incentives, conflict, and your ability to attract investors.
→
Equal splits work best when all founders are full-time from day 1
→
Cap table: who owns what — founders, angels, VCs, ESOP pool
→
Dilution: each funding round reduces your % but grows total value
🎯
Use a cap table tool from day 1. Mismanaged equity is one of the top reasons co-founder splits happen.
10
Deals
Negotiation Skills
Every founder negotiates constantly: with investors, hires, customers, and co-founders. Most are underprepared.
→
BATNA: know your best alternative before any negotiation
→
Silence is a tool: don't fill the pause after making an offer
→
Anchor high: the first number sets the range in both parties' minds
🎯
Create competition: "We're in conversations with others" shifts the power dynamic even if it's early.
11
Fundraising
Types of Investors
Not all money is the same. Who you take money from shapes your company's direction for years.
→
Angels: personal capital, early stage, less control
→
Seed/Micro VCs: £100k–£2M, Series Seed, niche expertise
→
VCs: large funds, board seats, high growth expectations
🎯
Strategic investors (corporates) can open enterprise doors but move slowly. Know the trade-off.
12
Legal
Legal Basics for Startups
Wrong legal structure or missed IP protection can kill a deal or destroy a company. Get this right early.
!
Incorporate early: Ltd/C-Corp protects you personally from day 1
!
IP assignment: all code and designs must be assigned to the company
!
Founder vesting: legally protect all co-founders with signed agreements
🎯
Spend £500 on a lawyer at incorporation. It saves £50,000 in disputes later.

Strategy & Growth

How startups survive, adapt, and accelerate

13
Growth
Go-to-Market Strategy
A GTM strategy answers: who buys this, how do we reach them, and what do we say to make them care?
→
ICP (Ideal Customer Profile): name the company, role, and pain
→
Channel: outbound, content, community, partnerships, or paid
→
Message: how do you describe the problem in the customer's language?
🎯
Pick one channel and go deep before trying another. Channel dilution kills early-stage GTM.
14
Growth
Growth Hacking
Growth hacking is systematic, data-driven experimentation to find scalable ways to acquire and retain users.
→
AARRR funnel: Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Referral → Revenue
→
Run 10 experiments a month: most fail, but you only need one to work
→
Viral loops: does the product generate referrals organically?
🎯
Fix retention before growth. A leaky bucket filled faster just empties faster.
15
Strategy
Pivoting
A pivot is a structured course correction — changing strategy while maintaining your mission and learnings.
→
Customer segment pivot: same product, different buyer
→
Problem pivot: same customer, different pain point to solve
→
Technology pivot: use what you built to solve a different problem
🎯
Pivot on strategy, not when things are just hard. Know the difference between a problem and an insight.
16
Product
Product Roadmap
A roadmap is not a feature list. It's a prioritised set of bets, each tied to a business outcome.
→
Now / Next / Later: a 3-horizon framework that avoids over-committing
→
Every item on the roadmap should have: outcome, owner, and evidence
→
Kill features that customers don't use — they have a maintenance cost
🎯
Say no to 80% of feature requests. The 20% you ship define your product's character.
17
Strategy
Competitive Moats
A moat is a durable competitive advantage. Without one, every big company can eventually copy you.
→
Network effects: product gets more valuable as more people use it
→
Data moats: proprietary data that improves with usage
→
Switching costs: deep integrations that make leaving painful
🎯
Brand and community are underrated moats for early-stage companies. Start building them now.
18
Endgame
Exit Strategies
Investors expect exits. Founders should understand the paths before signing a term sheet.
→
Acquisition: a larger company buys you. Most common startup exit
→
IPO: public listing. Requires scale, profitability path, and reporting capability
→
Secondary: founders or early investors sell shares to later-stage investors
🎯
Build relationships with potential acquirers before you need them. M&A is a relationship business.

People & Resilience

The human side of building a company

19
People
Conflict Resolution
Co-founder conflict is the #2 startup killer. Most disputes are about unspoken expectations — resolve them before they become crises.
→
Name the issue early. Ignored conflict compounds like bad debt
→
Separate people from positions: what do they actually need?
→
Get a neutral third party for equity or role disputes
🎯
Write down roles, decision rights, and expectations at incorporation — before any conflict exists.
20
Resilience
Founder Mental Health
Founders face loneliness, pressure, and uncertainty at a level few jobs match. Sustainability is a strategy.
✓
Separate self-worth from company performance — they are not the same
✓
Build a peer group of other founders — they understand in a way investors don't
✓
Protect recovery time: burnout is a threat to the company, not a badge of honour
🎯
The longest-lasting founders treat their energy as a resource to manage, not a cost to ignore.
