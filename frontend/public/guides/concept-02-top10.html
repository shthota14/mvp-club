Top 10 Things Every
Founder Must Do

From the first spark to your first paying customer — the non-negotiable moves, in order.

The founder's checklist — in sequence
💡 Phase 1 — Think
1
Must-do #1
Write your idea in one sentence
Before anything else — can you explain what you're building, for whom, and why, in a single clear sentence? If you can't, it's not ready.
✓ "I help [who] do [what] so they can [outcome]"
2
Must-do #2
Identify the exact problem — not the symptom
Most founders solve surface symptoms. Dig until you can answer: "What is the real pain, when does it happen, and what does it cost the person right now?"
✓ Ask "why?" 5 times until you hit root cause
💬 Phase 2 — Talk
3
Must-do #3
Talk to 10 real potential users — before building
Not friends. Not family. Find strangers who actually have the problem. Ask about their experience, not your solution. Listen more than you speak.
✓ Rule: 0 pitching, 100% listening
4
Must-do #4
Find the signal: 3 people who say "I'd pay for this"
Not "this sounds interesting." You need people who say they'd pay, or who would switch from their current solution. That's your green light to proceed.
✓ "Would you pay £X for this?" — watch the reaction
📋 Phase 3 — Plan
5
Must-do #5
Complete your Business Model Canvas
Map out all 9 blocks — who you serve, what you offer, how you reach them, what it costs, and how you make money. Do this before you touch any code or design.
✓ Available inside MVP Club → My Idea Vault
6
Must-do #6
Define your MVP in exactly 3 features
Not 10 features. Not a "complete product." What is the absolute minimum that lets a user solve their problem? Three features max. Cut everything else ruthlessly.
✓ If it's not core to the promise, cut it
🛠 Phase 4 — Build
7
Must-do #7
Build fast, ship ugly, learn fast
Perfection is the enemy of shipped. Your first version will be embarrassing — that's fine. Ship it to real users, get feedback, and iterate. Speed of learning beats quality of launch.
✓ "If you're not embarrassed, you launched too late"
8
Must-do #8
Tell everyone — distribution is the hardest part
Building is 20% of the job. Getting it in front of the right people is 80%. Post on Reddit, LinkedIn, communities, forums. Tell your network. Do things that don't scale.
✓ The best product without distribution = nothing
🚀 Phase 5 — Grow
9
Must-do #9
Get your first paying customer — not a free user
Free users lie. Paying customers vote with their wallet. Chase your first pound/dollar with urgency — it validates everything and proves you have a real business, not a hobby.
✓ Charge from day one, even if it's a small amount
10
Must-do #10
Build a feedback loop and never stop iterating
Talk to customers weekly. Track what they use, what they ignore, and what they complain about. The companies that win are the ones that listen and adapt fastest.
✓ Weekly user calls → monthly product update cycle
