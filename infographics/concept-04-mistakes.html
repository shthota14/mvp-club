The 8 mistakes — and how to avoid them
💻
Mistake #1
Building before talking to customers
Spending 6 months building in secret, then discovering nobody needs what you made. The classic "build it and they will come" trap.
⚡
Fix: Talk to 10 potential customers before writing a single line of code.
🌍
Mistake #2
Trying to solve for everyone
"Anyone with a phone could use this!" If your target market is "everyone", you're targeting no one. Broad = weak message, weak product, no traction.
⚡
Fix: Get specific. Name your ideal first customer. Build for them first, then expand.
🏗
Mistake #3
Over-engineering the MVP
Building every feature before launch. Adding the perfect onboarding, the dashboard, the API integrations — before you've validated anything.
⚡
Fix: MVP = the smallest thing that proves your core assumption. Ship it.
🤫
Mistake #4
Keeping the idea secret
Afraid someone will steal your idea, so you tell nobody. The cost of secrecy is zero feedback, zero network, and zero early adopters.
⚡
Fix: Ideas are worthless without execution. Share early. Execution is your moat.
💰
Mistake #5
Waiting to charge money
"I'll figure out monetization later." Free users don't validate a business. Only people who pay with real money are real validation signals.
⚡
Fix: Ask for money in week 1. Even a deposit. Payment is the ultimate validation.
👤
Mistake #6
Founding solo without realizing it
Going solo without building any support structure — no advisors, no community, no accountability. Startup loneliness is a real killer of momentum.
⚡
Fix: Join a community (like MVP Club). Find a co-founder or an accountability partner.
📊
Mistake #7
Mistaking activity for progress
Attending 10 startup events. Tweaking the landing page. Redesigning the logo. Busy ≠ moving forward. None of this gets you a customer.
⚡
Fix: Each week, ask: "Did I talk to a customer or get closer to revenue?" If no — reset.
🔄
Mistake #8
Pivoting too fast (or not at all)
Either quitting after the first "no" or ignoring 10 "nos" and charging ahead anyway. Both extremes destroy startups at different speeds.
⚡
Fix: Set clear criteria upfront: "If X doesn't happen after 10 interviews, I pivot." Then stick to it.

✅ The antidote: do these 3 things instead

🗣
Talk first, build second
Spend your first weeks having conversations, not writing code. Understanding beats assumption every time.
🎯
Ruthlessly narrow your focus
One problem. One customer segment. One solution. Nail it before you scale it. Specific beats broad.
🚀
Ship embarrassingly early
If you're not embarrassed by your first version, you waited too long. Real feedback beats perfect plans.
