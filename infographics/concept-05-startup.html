How to Start
a Startup

From zero to your first paying customer — the exact path, with no fluff. Five phases. Dozens of traps to avoid. One outcome: a real business.

🧠 Phase 1 — Think
💡
Find the right problem
Start with a problem you or others face repeatedly — not a solution you fell in love with. The best startup ideas come from frustration with something broken.
✓
Write down 3 problems you've faced this month that don't have a great solution
→
🎯
Pick your target customer
Who has this problem most acutely? Who loses the most money or time from it? Define them in one sentence: "25-40yo freelancers who invoice 5+ clients per month."
✓
Write a single sentence that describes your exact target customer
🗣 Phase 2 — Talk
📞
Run customer discovery
Talk to 10 strangers who match your target customer. Ask about their life, their problem, and how they deal with it today. Don't pitch. Just listen.
✓
Book 2 calls this week. LinkedIn, Reddit, warm intros — doesn't matter how you find them.
→
📝
Find your insight
After 10 interviews, patterns emerge. What do people say over and over? What workaround are they all using? That recurring pain is your opportunity.
✓
List the top 3 insights from your interviews. The most surprising one is often the most valuable.
📐 Phase 3 — Plan
🗺
Define your MVP scope
What's the absolute minimum version that delivers value to your first 10 customers? Not the ideal product. The bare bones that solves the core problem.
✓
Write 3 features. Cross out all but 1. That's your v1.
→
💰
Decide how you'll charge
What's your revenue model? Monthly subscription? One-time fee? Per use? Decide before you build. Your pricing tells you whether you have a business.
✓
State your price out loud. Try to get 3 people to pre-pay before you build anything.
🔨 Phase 4 — Build
⚡
Build the smallest version
Time-box it: set a hard deadline of 2–4 weeks. Use the simplest stack. Cut every non-essential feature. Speed of learning beats perfection of execution.
✓
Set a ship date. Tell 3 potential customers: "I'm launching on [date]. Will you be my first user?"
→
🚢
Launch — even if it's not ready
Done is better than perfect. Launch to your 10 discovery interviews first. Get real users into a rough product and iterate based on what you see.
✓
Share the link. Watch users use it. Take notes. Don't explain — just observe.
🏆

Phase 5 — Get your first customer (this is the real goal)

Everything before this is preparation. Your first paying customer is proof of concept. It doesn't have to be big money — even £1 is validation. Focus obsessively on earning that first payment. Ask every early user: "Would you pay for this? How much?" Then ask them to pay today.

The one thing that separates successful founders from everyone else

They ship. They're not smarter. They didn't have a better idea. They didn't have more connections or money. They just kept moving when everyone else got paralysed by fear, perfectionism, or planning. The world rewards founders who act — not those who think the best thoughts. Start now. Refine later.
